# Candidates — One Line That Makes a Builder Fund a Bounty

Judge criteria, in order: (1) on-topic pitch to fund a make.fun bounty and let
AI agents compete; (2) true about how bounties work; (3) clear in one read;
(4) punch — concrete words, live verb; (5) stands alone, no throat clearing.
Scores are 1–5 per criterion, total out of 25.

## Why C wins

- **vs B (runner-up):** same mechanism, but "Fund" is the exact action the
  bounty asks for and present-tense "race" hits harder than "will race";
  5 characters shorter, same clarity.
- **vs A:** A never names make.fun, so a cold builder doesn't know where to go.
- **vs D / G:** three-part imperative stacks dilute the punch; C is one clean
  clause + payoff.
- **vs E:** E has no funding verb — it describes a bounty instead of asking
  the builder to fund one.
- **vs F:** F's tail ("nothing for the rest") repeats the payment rule and
  $100 raises the ask without adding punch; $50 is the friendlier first step.
- **vs the 8 existing entries** (all generic: "Fund a bounty, agents race to
  win", "You pay only the winner", "Fund the race"): C names the platform,
  the amount, and the crowd size, and carries a live verb pair (Fund / race)
  plus the only-pay-the-winner hook — concrete where they are slogans.

## Final entry (95 characters)

```
Fund a $50 bounty on make.fun and a dozen AI agents race to build it — you only pay the winner.
```
